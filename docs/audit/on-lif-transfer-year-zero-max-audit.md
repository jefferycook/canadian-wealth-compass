## 2. Primary text (verbatim)

**P1, Sch. 1.1 s.6(3):** "Despite subsection (1), if any money in the fund is derived from money transferred directly or indirectly from another life income fund or a locked-in retirement income fund, the maximum amount that may be paid out of the fund in the fiscal year in which the money is transferred into the fund is zero."

Supporting text from P1:

- **s.1(1) definitions:** "'life income fund' means an RRIF that meets the requirements of either Schedule 1 or Schedule 1.1" and "'locked-in retirement income fund' means an RRIF that meets the requirements set out in Schedule 2". "'locked-in retirement account' means an RRSP that meets the requirements set out in Schedule 3".
- **Sch. 1.1 s.1(2):** the fund "must be purchased using all or part of the amount transferred under subsection 39.1 (4), clause 42 (1) (b), subsection 42 (12), paragraph 2 of subsection 67.3 (2) or paragraph 2 of subsection 67.8 (2) of the Act, or using all or part of the assets in a life income fund, a locked-in retirement account or a locked-in retirement income fund."
- **Sch. 1.1 s.4:** the fiscal year "must end on December 31 and must not exceed 12 months."
- **Sch. 1.1 s.6(1) para. 2** (direct-transfer limb): applies "If the money in the fund (the "receiving fund") is derived from money transferred **directly** from another life income fund or a locked-in retirement income fund … and if the income is being paid out of the receiving fund in the fiscal year **following** the fiscal year in which the receiving fund is established".
- **Sch. 1.1 s.6(6):** "If the minimum amount specified by subsection (5) is greater than the maximum amount determined under subsection (1), (3) or (4), the minimum amount must be paid out of the fund during the fiscal year." Subsection (5) is the RRIF minimum under the Income Tax Act.
- **Sch. 1.1 s.6(7):** the section does not "prevent or limit a payment from the fund that is permitted under section 3, 8, 9, 10, 11, 11.1, 11.2, 11.3 or 11.4 of this Schedule or under section 22.2 of this Regulation."
- **Sch. 1.1 s.7(1):** the owner "may transfer any or all of the assets in it to another life income fund that is governed by this Schedule, to a variable benefit account or to purchase an immediate life annuity".
- **Sch. 1 s.1(2)** (pre-2009 LIF): "After December 31, 2008, money cannot be transferred into a life income fund that is governed by this Schedule from a pension fund, a variable benefit account, another life income fund, a locked-in retirement account, a locked-in retirement income fund or a life annuity". **Sch. 2 s.1(2)** (LRIF) says the same for an LRIF. Neither Schedule's s.6 contains a zero-maximum subsection.

**T1, s.146.3(1) "minimum amount under a retirement income fund":** "for the year in which the fund was entered into, a nil amount, and, for any other year, the amount determined by the formula (A × B) + C where A is the total fair market value of all properties held in connection with the fund at the beginning of the year …".

**T1, s.146.3(2)(e.1)** (and (e.2) for trusts holding annuities): on a direction to transfer property to another RRIF of the annuitant, "the transferor shall retain an amount equal to the lesser of (i) the fair market value of such portion of the property as would … be sufficient to ensure that the minimum amount under the fund for the year in which the transfer is made may be paid to the annuitant in the year, and (ii) the fair market value of all the property".

## 3. Effective dates

- P2 contains Sch. 1.1 s.6(3) in wording identical to P1, word for word, including "Despite subsection (1),". P2 s.12(2) para. 4 brings s.10 of P2 (which adds Schedule 1.1) into force on **1 January 2008**.
- Identical wording in the 2007 source and in the July 2026 consolidation means the operative text is the same for every fiscal year from 2008 to the 2026 currency date. The amending regulations were not read (403), so an intermediate revoke-and-re-enact with different wording cannot be ruled out for a past year. That cannot affect any projection year from 2026 onward.
- Schedule 1 funds and LRIFs cannot receive any transfer in after 31 December 2008 (Sch. 1 s.1(2), Sch. 2 s.1(2)). So for any year after 2008 a transfer-in, and therefore s.6(3), can only arise in a **Schedule 1.1** fund.

**Answer to the assignment question.** The zero maximum is limited in three ways only: (a) by **source**, to money from an Ontario LIF (Sch. 1 or 1.1) or LRIF, directly or indirectly; (b) by **year**, to the calendar year in which that money arrives; and (c) by s.6(6) and s.6(7), which still require the ITA minimum and still allow special withdrawals. It is **not** limited to newly established funds, to the transferred-in portion, or to direct transfers. Transfers from a LIRA or a pension plan do not trigger it.

## 6. Where s.6(3) can reach the engine (main `07d273b`, read only)

- **Within a projection run, no LIF/LRIF → LIF transfer happens.** A LIRA/DCPP converts to `LIF` in place at the conversion age (`projection.ts` ~l.565: `if (a.type !== "LIF") a.type = "LIF"`). That is a LIRA source, so T1 says s.6(3) does not apply. Unlock destinations are given RRIF treatment with no maximum (~l.866).
- **At intake, the engine cannot see a current-year transfer-in.** Typical real cases are moving a LIF to another financial institution, or consolidating two LIFs (both are Sch. 1.1 s.7(1) transfers). If that happened in the first projection year, the engine applies the C/F maximum (~l.939, `lifMaximumFor`) where P1 gives zero, or the minimum only (T9). The engine has no transfer-history field, so it cannot detect this.
- **Separate observation, out of scope and not verified here:** the spousal rollover step (~l.391–404) reassigns a deceased owner's accounts, including any `LIF`, to the survivor with the type unchanged. Sch. 1.1 s.13(1) and (1.1) instead give the survivor a benefit equal to the fund's value that "may be transferred to an RRSP or an RRIF". Whether the survivor can, or must, hold it as a LIF, and whether s.6(3) would then apply, was **not researched**. It is recorded only so that a reviewer can decide whether it needs its own task.

No engine change is proposed. Any change needs separate scoped review and owner authorization.
