## 2. Primary text (P1, verbatim)

**Schedule 1 (LIFs that "cannot be purchased after December 31, 2008"), s.6(1):** "must not exceed the greater of "A" and "B" where, "A" is the amount of the investment earnings, including any unrealized capital gains or losses, of the fund in the previous fiscal year, and "B" is the amount calculated using the formula, C/F". The section has **no** first-year proration subsection and **no** transfer-year-zero subsection.

**Schedule 1.1 (current LIFs), s.6:**

- (1) "must not exceed the greatest of the following amounts:
  1. The investment earnings, including any unrealized capital gains or losses, of the fund in the previous fiscal year.
  2. If the money in the fund (the "receiving fund") is derived from money transferred directly from another life income fund or a locked-in retirement income fund (the "transferring fund"), and if the income is being paid out of the receiving fund in the fiscal year following the fiscal year in which the receiving fund is established, the sum of, i. the investment earnings … of the transferring fund in the previous fiscal year, and ii. the investment earnings … of the receiving fund in the previous fiscal year.
  3. The amount calculated using the formula, C/F"
- C = "the value of the assets in the fund at the beginning of the fiscal year". F = "the present value, at the beginning of the fiscal year, of an annuity of $1 payable annually in advance over the period commencing at the beginning of the fiscal year and ending on December 31 of the year in which the owner reaches 90 years of age."
- (2) The rate for each of the first 15 fiscal years is "the greater of 6 per cent and the nominal rate of interest on long-term bonds issued by the Government of Canada for November of the year before the beginning of the fiscal year … CANSIM series V122487". For the 16th and later years it is 6 per cent.
- (3) "if any money in the fund is derived from money transferred directly or indirectly from another life income fund or a locked-in retirement income fund, the maximum amount that may be paid out of the fund in the fiscal year in which the money is transferred into the fund is zero."
- (4) "If the initial fiscal year of the fund is not 12 months long, the maximum amount determined under subsection (1) shall be adjusted in proportion to the number of months in that fiscal year divided by 12, with any part of an incomplete month counting as one month."
- (5)/(6): payments must be at least the RRIF minimum under the Income Tax Act. If that minimum exceeds the maximum under (1), (3) or (4), the minimum must be paid.
- (7): the section does not limit payments permitted under ss.3, 8, 9, 10, 11, 11.1–11.4 of the Schedule or s.22.2 of the Regulation. These are unlocking and other special withdrawals, which are out of scope here.

**Both schedules, s.4:** the fiscal year "must end on December 31 and must not exceed 12 months."

**Schedule 2 (LRIF, "cannot be purchased after December 31, 2008")**: s.6 has the same greater-of-A-and-B structure as Schedule 1. The engine has no LRIF account type, so Schedule 2 is noted only for completeness.

## 3. Independent re-derivation of the C/F limb

The fiscal year is the calendar year (s.4), and F runs from the start of the fiscal year to 31 December of the calendar year in which the owner reaches 90. F therefore depends only on **a = the age the owner reaches in the calendar year**, which needs the birth year but not the birth date. The number of annual payments in advance is `n = 90 − a + 1`.

At a flat 6%, `F = Σ_{k=0}^{n−1} 1.06^−k` and the limb is `100/F` %. Computed in Python `Decimal` (40 digits) and rounded half-up to five decimals, **all 50 rows for ages 41–90 equal `ON_LIF_MAX` in `src/lib/planning/registered.ts` at `main` 07d273b, with 0 mismatches.** Spot values: 41 → 5.98531, 55 → 6.45234, 65 → 7.25513, 71 → 8.22496, 80 → 11.96160, 89 → 51.45631, 90 → 100.00000.

This corroborates the FSRA audit in #39 comment 6642084654 from the regulation itself rather than from FSRA's table. It also **confirms from statute that the table key is the age reached in the calendar year.** #67 left this as an open question, and under it the direction in §5 holds.

Assumption still open: the 6% floor binding for 2026 depends on V122487 for November 2025 being ≤ 6% (B1, not read). If that rate were above 6%, every row with n ≤ 15 (ages 76–90) would differ, and so would the first 15 discount steps of every other row.

**Interaction order (Sch.1.1), as read from the text:** `max = greatest(D5, D6 if eligible, D1)`, replaced by 0 in a D7 year. Otherwise, in an initial fiscal year shorter than 12 months, scale by months/12 under D8. Then pay at least the RRIF minimum under D9. Whether the months/12 factor applies to all three limbs or only C/F is not stated beyond "the maximum amount determined under subsection (1)". On a plain reading it applies to the greatest of the three. A reviewer should confirm this.

## 5. RRIF minimum age basis versus LIF key

- **R1, s.7308(4)**, verbatim: the factor corresponds to "the age in whole years (in the table referred to as "Y") attained by the individual at the beginning of the year". It is `1/(90 − Y)` under 71, then the table from 71 to 95+. Qualifying RRIFs use s.7308(3), which the engine does not model. R2 gives a nil minimum in the year the fund is entered into (per #67, not re-read here).
- **The LIF key (D2) is the age reached during the calendar year**, by statute (§3). For anyone not born on 1 January, `Y = a − 1`.
- `projection.ts` passes the same row age to `rrifMinFactor(age)` and `lifMaximumFor(…, age, …)`. If the row age is `a`, which the LIF table requires, then the RRIF factor is one row too old and the **minimum is overstated**. Example: row 71 uses 5.28%, but the statutory value for Y = 70 is 1/20 = 5.00%. #67 §3 tabulates more rows. With the LIF key now fixed by statute, this resolves #67's "either reading" ambiguity: **the RRIF lookup is the misaligned one.** A 1 January birthday is an edge case: the person is arguably Y = a at the beginning of the year. A reviewer should confirm that.
- The existing `rrif.ageBasisWholeYear` `APPROXIMATE` status already discloses a whole-year proxy. This file records the direction of the error but proposes **no engine change**. A fix needs a separate scoped review and owner authorization. Note that the fix needs only the birth year for the LIF, but the RRIF needs the birth date, or at least a 1 January flag.

## 6. Fixture requirements before any methodology change

No numbers here are approved. Each fixture must cite its P1/R1 provision and carry hand arithmetic.

1. **D1/D2**: birth year, valuation year and Jan-1 FMV. Cases: a = 41, 71, 89 and 90. Also re-run the 50-row derivation if B1 (the November rate) ever exceeds 6%.
2. **D3**: an owner aged 40 or below. This documents the current overstatement and requires an explicit decision: extend the formula, or refuse.
3. **D5**: prior-year earnings, including unrealized gains, that exceed C/F (e.g. a large unrealized gain at age 60). Plus the opposite case.
4. **D6**: a Sch.1.1 fund established in year t by direct LIF→LIF transfer, paying in t+1. It needs both funds' year-t earnings, plus a negative-control case for t+2.
5. **D7**: a transfer-in during year t. The maximum is 0 in t and the RRIF minimum still applies (D9). Cover both direct and indirect transfers.
6. **D8**: a Sch.1.1 fund established 15 March (10 months, March counts as a full month) without a LIF/LRIF transfer-in, so D7 does not apply. Expected value is max × 10/12. Plus a Sch.1 negative control with no proration.
7. **RRIF**: a mid-year birthday at a = 71 (Y = 70 → 5.00%), a 1 January birthday, the 94→95 transition, and an establishment year (nil minimum).

Fixtures 3–6 need inputs that the engine does not currently collect: account schedule, establishment date, transfer history and prior-year earnings. Those are product and methodology decisions for the owner.
